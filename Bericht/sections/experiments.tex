\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Standard-DQN}
\label{subsec:exp-standard-dqn}

For each \acs{drausp} benchmark instance, we train the standard DQN baseline from
Section~\ref{sec:rl-approach} -- a plain feedforward network (32~$\to$~16 hidden units) with no
structural knowledge of the monotonicity relations from Section~\ref{subsec:monotonicity} -- using
action masking, the target-network soft update, and the reject-biased $\epsilon$-greedy exploration
described earlier.

\textbf{Parameters.} Unless noted otherwise we use reject-bias $\alpha=0.75$, $\epsilon$ decaying from 
$1.0$ to $0.1$ over $20{,}000$ steps, a replay buffer of $50{,}000$ transitions with a $500$-step warm-up, 
batch size $64$ and one training step every $4$ environment steps. We evaluate the agent's monotonicity
behavior every $100$ training episodes by sampling $5$ trajectories, creating pairs of states as described 
in Section~\ref{subsec:monotonicity}, and counting the number of monotonicity violations in these pairs.

One can see one example of the training results in Figure~\ref{fig:standard_dqn} for the first $2000$ epsiodes.
\begin{figure}[ht]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{grafiken/Experiments/standard_DQN_mono.png}
	\caption{Standard-DQN and its monotonicity ratios for SB05, $T_d = 50$ and the first $2000$ episodes}
	\label{fig:standard_dqn}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Monotonicity behavior.} The Standard-DQN without any structural knowledge about the
problem, still manages to learn a policy that is mostly monotone, with only a few violations. Especially
the monotonicity in $C_k$ is learned very well, and is fulfilled after few training episodes already.
Monotonicity in $q$ shows an interesting behavior: first the monotonicity drops to a ratio of $0$,
however during training the monotonicity recovers and gets to a ratio of $>0.9$ at the end. This behaviour can
be seen across different instances.

\textbf{Hier nochmal drüberschauen wies am Ende aussieht.}

The monotonicity in $t$ and $r$ is learned well from the beginning on and achieves a ratio $>0.9$ in the end.
For the mixed pairs the monotonicity is learned very well from the beginning on.


\textbf{Evluation.} After training, the agent's performance is evaluated. Note that the network weights does not
change anymore here. We report the average performance on $10{,}000$ episodes with $\epsilon = 0$.

\subsection{Lattice-DQN}
The Lattice-DQN is built up exactly as described in Section~\ref{subsec:lattice-architecture}.

\textbf{Parameters.} We use $P=8$ keypoints for the calibrators, and ensemble widths $U_1=4, U_2=2$ for the two layers of lattices.
The parameters for th training scheme are the same as fr the Standard-DQN.

Not surprsingly the monotonicity behavior is perfect from the beginning on, as the network architecture enforces monotonicity by design.
Nevertheless, the training is slower, even compared with a Standard-DQN with the same number of parameters, as the network is more complex and has to learn the same policy with a more constrained function class.
